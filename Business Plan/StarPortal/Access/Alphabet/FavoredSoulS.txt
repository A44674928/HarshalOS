Hidden Layers in Strategy

    Subterfuge and Deception: Hidden layers can involve elements of subterfuge, where the true intentions or capabilities are concealed until the right moment. This can catch opponents off guard and provide a strategic advantage.

    Multiple Facets: Having hidden layers means that there are multiple facets to a plan or character, allowing for flexibility and adaptability. This can be particularly useful in dynamic situations where conditions change rapidly.

    Depth and Complexity: Hidden layers add depth to the narrative, making it more engaging and thought-provoking. They can reveal new insights and perspectives as they are uncovered.

Example Scenario

Imagine the heir using hidden layers in their strategy to counter the meddlers:

    Decoy Operations: The heir might set up decoy operations to mislead the meddlers, drawing their attention away from the true objective.

    Hidden Allies: They could have hidden allies or resources that are revealed at a critical moment, turning the tide of the conflict.

    Secret Plans: The heir might have a secret plan that only a few trusted advisors know about, ensuring that it remains hidden from the meddlers until it's too late for them to react.
