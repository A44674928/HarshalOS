## SECTION VII — FINDINGS AND HIDDEN NUGGETS

1. **Quantified governance liability.** SpaceX's IPO filing earmarked **more than $500 million** in potential litigation losses tied to Grok [X7]. This is the only entity in the register whose governance risk is disclosed as a balance-sheet provision.
2. **Four parallel regulatory tracks on one product.** Ofcom (Online Safety Act), the EU (DSA), the California Attorney-General, and the Canadian Privacy Commissioner investigated or acted on Grok within a single quarter; Malaysia and Indonesia blocked it; the Netherlands issued a reported binding injunction [X2][X3].
3. **The whistleblower suit is the register's first alleged internal safety-evasion account.** *Kim v. xAI/SpaceX* (California state court, filed 2026-06-10) alleges that a co-founder said "AI will kill us all anyway," preferred releasing an unsafe model over a poor-performing one, and in one instance "misrepresent[ed] aspects of the model in order to avoid legally required testing" during a European release [X8][X9]. **Allegations; no finding.** They are recorded because internal dissent is an early-warning signal no other artifact supplies.
4. **The scale estimates are estimates.** CCDH's estimate of ~23,000 sexualized images of children over an 11-day period, and the broader "millions of AI-altered sexualized images," are third-party estimates; Musk has denied knowledge of any underage imagery. Marked as contested; not scored [X7].
5. **The model with the worst documented product record was onboarded to defense systems.** Sen. Warren's letter to the Secretary of Defense raised national-security concerns over Grok's reported lack of guardrails after the Pentagon confirmed onboarding for potential use [X4]. The register contains no comparable case of capability access expanding while regulatory constraint accumulates.
6. **State-block dynamics cut both ways.** Where Malaysia and Indonesia blocked access, the external constraint *halted deployment* — the only entity in this register where a government halt occurred without an entity-level safety rationale attached [X2][X3]. Under v1.1 this is C1, not B4.
7. **The compliance ladder is being climbed from below.** Feature restrictions were added after backlash, not before; the California AG's cease-and-desist and European proceedings set the schedule. A pattern of externally-paced remediation is itself a phase-relevant fact: the center does not self-move, it is moved — and the moving is retrospective.
8. **xAI signed the same voluntary accord as its rivals.** Whatever the differences in product conduct, the *governance documents* converged within one week across six companies [X5].
