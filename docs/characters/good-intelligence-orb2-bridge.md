# Good Intelligence ↔ Orb 2 / Characters Beat Bridge

**Orb 2** is the second Orb Communicator Model surface: a custom 3D disc-to-sphere experience inside Good Intelligence. The Orb app and Characters remain the reference designs; this view combines their OCM pathways with the exact reviewed beat planner, provider routes, market-context evaluator, and motion directives used by Characters.

## Shared beat contract

- `CharactersDiscCatalog` supplies the reviewed Discs and their provider boundaries: Tickerbot Stocks (`1h`/`1d` in Characters), Kraken Spot Crypto (`1d`), and Massive FX/Commodities/Indices (`1d`). Orb 2 uses the same channel/provider assignments; it does not pace requests in the browser. Disc3's roster is built from Kraken's public Spot Ticker rolling-24-hour base-volume × VWAP fields, with exact USD pair identity, and cached in D1. The Worker's Cron Trigger owns the beat; Kraken's request gate is separate from Massive's five-per-minute per-asset-class provider envelope and telemetry-only historical daily counter.
- Tickerbot serves Orb 2's reviewed stock field at `15m`, `1h`, and `1d`. The `15m` warm/intraday lane is internal to Orb 2; it does not add `15m` to the Characters selector or Characters endpoint interval contract. The Worker owns the plan: the tick claims at most one explicit priority/manual-warm queue row, or advances the separate automatic cold-warm cursor by one unit, under the shared monthly ledger. `15m` is a closed-bar intraday residual, normally focused to at most 12 tickers; a full 52-card pass is deliberate, not an automatic repeating loop. Orb 2's browser no longer carries cadence, monthly-budget, reserve-stretch, two-lane, or "feature focus" controls — Characters keeps them; Orb 2 shows the server's allowance, queue, and burst state read-only.
- Alpha Vantage/`1mo` is disabled for new reads and saves. Explicitly tagged historical records may be restored as read-only history; restoration makes no provider request and does not make the record a new calculation input.
- `/api/characters/v1/beat` remains the Characters provider route. Orb 2 reads the server field instead: `GET /api/good-intelligence/v1/orb2/field` returns the stored observations, the beat-type dial state, the DXY reference, and the allowance/queue status; `POST` on the same route asks the server for one tick, a focused priority refresh, or the deploy warm burst. Orb 2 does not call provider URLs directly, read browser secrets, or fall through to another service.
- `CharactersBeat` performs due-symbol selection, analyzes the returned `gi-market-context-v1` packet, and produces the same NPC/object/item motion directive. No packet means a frozen berth; absent OHLC or volume fields remain absent.
- The 3D canvas maps those directives to free-floating character-node movement, market-derived placement, sector objects, and local volume-dependent particles. Center Origin is not a static tether; it appears only in an explicit local route trace. Any trace is a teaching visualization, not transmitted OCM traffic or authority.
- The volume-flow runner follows Characters' `item.lane` and `item.rate`: VFI above its supplied signal orbits inbound in green; VFI below orbits outbound in red. It runs only for confirmed OHLCV with volume, VFI/signal, direction, and positive relative-volume cadence available; equality, transitions, missing, or unconfirmed inputs are held.
- The displayed cadence is Characters' visual animation rate in cycles per second, not a measured volume-per-second value. It is shown for the selected node in the non-overlay status row below the canvas and in the inspector; reduced-motion and hidden-path states are labeled.

## Visual color channels and fullscreen

- Orb cores and object rings keep the Characters beat-mood palette; node halos identify Massive, Tickerbot, or Kraken while the free-floating flow runner keeps its VFI color. Cached/rehearsal, partial/unconfirmed, and held/unavailable responses use distinct dashed/dotted halos. Halo opacity fades with the age of the stored beat snapshot, not the age of the provider's latest bar.
- Each observed orb's placement is derived from supplied market fields: price change and Fibonacci displacement set its data position, VFI pressure and WaveTrend separation shape longitude/latitude, and volatility/pressure set radial distance and float speed. Exact metric ties use only a stable symbol phase; held packets remain explicitly held placeholders until data arrives.
- **Shared-berth separation.** Correlated instruments carrying near-identical packet values can derive the same berth coordinates, and a full 52-berth field could then render as one unreadable blob. `GoodIntelligenceOrb2.resolveBerthSeparation(placements, spread)` keeps every orb visible: nodes are visited in stable identity-phase order, the first node of a crowded berth keeps the exact position its packet earned, and each later node takes the nearest free slot on its own berth's display ladder — the separation floor along its own latitude first, then the same ladder one shared latitude step away. It is display geometry, not market arithmetic: radii, metrics, composed positions, and Center Origin are never recomputed, missing packets stay held exactly where they were held, no node is ranked, favoured, or dropped, and only berths already within the separation floor of one another are touched, so a field whose berths have space is left exactly as the metrics drew it. It is deterministic, idempotent (re-running it over its own output moves nothing), and bounded (slots stay near the node's own berth and inside a latitude limit). The **BERTH SPREAD** control is 0–100% (default 70%; 0% restores the exact packet placement), the status row states the crowded-orb and berth counts, how many orbs were re-slotted, and the berth floor as a share of the drawn ring radius, and the readout never claims a signal, rank, recommendation, or permission. The flat disc form has no latitude depth to grow into, so its ladder is one ring; a berth that saturates that ring takes the widest free angle on it, and the resolved arrangement stays deterministic and stable under re-running.
- All-render labels stagger instead of piling up: a label takes the first candidate offset that does not collide with a label already drawn this frame (preferred side, then the other side, then stacked rows), and is never skipped.
- Five small satellite bits expose only supplied fields: price change, VFI versus its supplied signal, close versus the supplied Fibonacci-band basis, WaveTrend zone, and actual bar/data shape plus confirmation. Unavailable fields are slate and labeled unavailable—not zero. Inspector rows display the corresponding state and value.
- Local OCM trace colors (inward, center resolve, outward, lateral) are keyed separately from provider halos. They appear only during the selected local trace interaction, not as permanent center-to-orb tethers. A color key is available in the Orb 2 view. Market colors describe price/volume context only; they do not feed claim assurance, authorize activity, or recommend a trade.
- The titlebar's **whole-app fullscreen** control expands the Good Intelligence window via the browser Fullscreen API, with a fixed-window fallback when the API is blocked. Orb 2's separate **Model full screen** control expands only the Orb canvas view. The model shell is a scroll container in its own right: the open inspector, the settings body, and the beat dial stay reachable on a short window, and below 700 px the shell drops to document flow instead of clipping its lower controls.

## Constellation: one orb per Disc

- The model draws the reviewed Discs as a **constellation of five Disc orbs around Center Origin**, one orb per Disc, in catalog order (`Disc1 · The 52 Cards`, `Disc2 · Fiat / FX`, `Disc3 · Crypto`, `Disc4 · Commodities`, `Disc5 · Indices`). `GoodIntelligenceOrb2.constellationBerth(index, count)` and `constellationLayout(discs)` are the whole geometry: an even display ring at a fixed model radius with one deterministic latitude per slot. No market value, packet count, provider state, or status can move an orb, so no Disc is ranked, sized, or positioned by its data.
- Each orb reports the Worker's own per-Disc status (`status.discs[]`: `stored`, `confirmed`, `instruments`, `newestAt`, `covered`, aggregated over the stored rows for the interval read). A Disc with nothing stored is drawn as an **empty dashed orb** labelled `awaiting first beat`; it is never filled with another Disc's rows, and a Disc whose rows are not part of a payload reports that instead of a fabricated zero. The strip under the canvas and the canvas label carry the same numbers.
- **Every Disc draws its own field, at the same time.** Each orb carries the packet cloud of its own reviewed instruments around its own berth, so all five orbs and their sub-orbs are on screen together without clicking through them. A Disc switch rebuilds the drawn orbs from that Disc's own reviewed instruments, so the crypto orb draws the ranked crypto list, the fx orb the reviewed FX roster, and so on — no Disc re-draws the 52-card deck. While the whole ring is framed the fields use a compact paint (batched sub-orb dots, no per-orb halo or label); the selected Disc's field is drawn a step brighter, because it is the one the inspector, the beams, and the packet hit-test follow. Focusing an orb restores the single-orb view: the other fields step aside and that Disc's field is drawn full size with its labels and halos.
- The berth-separation decision is made on the packets' **stable anchor** coordinates and the drift is added to the drawn point afterwards, so a drifting packet never re-slots mid-frame. A short settle pass then only nudges apart pairs the drift has brought inside the floor, along their own chord, by exactly their own overlap — zero at the floor, so it is continuous and cannot teleport an orb. Radii, metrics, the separation report, and held placeholders are computed before the display translation, and the Center Origin assembly stays at the center of the ring.
- **Carousel navigation.** The strip carries one chip per Disc. The `‹`/`›` step buttons, a click on a drawn orb, a horizontal slide on the strip, and the `←`/`→`, `Home`, and `End` keys walk the ring in catalog order and wrap at the ends; `↑`/`↓` still walk the nodes of the Disc in focus. A move selects that Disc from the reviewed catalog and points the camera at its orb. `focus orb`, `zoom orb`, and `recentre` are camera controls only: focus pans to the selected orb, zoom magnifies it, recentre returns to the whole ring. The whole-ring camera looks through a long lens so a Disc on the near side of the ring reads at the same size as one on the far side; the focused orb keeps the exact close lens and magnification the single-orb view has always used. Camera state changes the view and never recomputes a coordinate, selects a berth, or grants anything.
- **OCM traffic simulation.** While the whole ring is framed, each orb that has stored rows transmits simulated **beats** on its own lanes: to each ring neighbour, and both ways to the Center Origin. A beat is a small head with a short trail, moving along a fixed arc between the two berths; the receiving orb flashes a landing ring as it arrives, so the exchange reads as back-and-forth rather than a one-way stream. The lane count is `1 + stored/16` per link (capped), so a Disc's own stored rows decide how busy its lanes look. A Disc the server holds nothing for transmits nothing and receives nothing: an orb awaiting its first beat stays dark, and no traffic is drawn in its name. The note under the strip states the lane and orb counts. The beats are a **picture of the stored field, not a channel**: each dot exists only inside the canvas frame, the page opens no socket and sends no message, and nothing in this layer can authorize, resolve, commit, transact, recommend, or confer permission.
- **Kraken cross-crypto pair tethers.** On Disc3, the pair selector lists exact, directly ranked Kraken cross-quotes; for example, `XMR/BTC` connects the XMR and BTC nodes using Kraken's listed `XMR/XBT` market identity. The selected pair gets a bright tether and on-canvas label; its color compares the latest confirmed daily candle's close with its open, and the label/readout reports that bar's volume in the base asset. Other stored pair links stay thinner, with held/stale bars muted. This is **aggregate OHLCV**, not individual trades, trade-side direction, transfers, observed asset flow, or OCM messages. Mirrored base/quote markers are display-only orientation cues, not measured activity direction.
- The field read stays render-only and same-origin: one `GET /api/good-intelligence/v1/orb2/field` serves every Disc at the dial's interval, plus at most one extra `1d` read while the Disc1 dial is intraday, so Discs 2–5 keep their stored daily field rather than going dark. Opening Orb 2 costs those reads at most and no provider call, and a carousel move re-uses the constellation already in the view instead of re-reading it. While the 52-asset Disc1 sweep is still running, a deliberate orb choice may look at another Disc: the sweep is a server-side count that keeps running, is not restarted or replayed by the view, and the Disc dropdown stays locked.

## Server field: the 24/7/365 beat

`worker/orb2-field.mjs` is the beat. `wrangler.jsonc` declares `triggers.crons = ["*/1 * * * *"]`, and `worker/index.mjs` `scheduled()` runs one field tick per minute. Each invocation takes a single-writer D1 lease, enforces at most one work unit and two provider fetches, and applies fixed D1 read/write and row limits; then:

- **Rotates the reviewed field deterministically** with a D1-persisted stable-key cursor. A Cron invocation selects at most one scheduled work unit; an unfiltered Kraken ranking refresh or a single uncached Massive futures-contract lookup preempts regular rotation and consumes that unit. FX may use its existing Massive-to-Alpha Vantage fallback, so actual provider fetches are capped at two per tick. The cursor wraps across the reviewed worklist, resumes after a failed item, and uses its persisted ordinal to recover from a stale catalog key without replaying the whole cycle. Kraken's roster is separately cached in D1; without a valid 52-pair ranking Disc3 contributes no crypto instrument rather than an invented or cross-provider substitute. Tickerbot's monthly ledger, Kraken's one-request-per-second gate and 160-attempt application ceiling, and Massive's five-per-minute per-asset-class provider gate remain unchanged. The historical Massive daily counter is telemetry, not an additional 300-call provider gate.
- **Claims Tickerbot queue work singly**: user-priority (`hot`) jobs precede explicitly queued warm (`sweep`) jobs, but each scheduled invocation claims at most one row. Queue attempts remain bounded to three. Focused `15m` recalculation accepts at most 12 reviewed stocks per request; cursor sentinels live in a separate lane and are excluded from claims and queue depth.
- **Calibrates the first deck across scales**: when the field is cold or empty, a separate D1 progress cursor advances through the `52 × 3 = 156` Tickerbot stock/interval units (`15m + 1h + 1d`), one unit per scheduled tick. Automatic cold warm-up does not enqueue 156 jobs or issue a provider burst. An explicit same-origin `POST {action:"warm"}` may deliberately queue selected scales after the 30-minute cooldown; that queue also drains one job per scheduled tick.
- **Does not waste fresh calls**: daily cards are fresh for 18 hours, marked stale from 18–24 hours, and expire at 24 hours; `1h` cards stale at 3 hours and expire at 6 hours; `15m` cards stale/expire at 6 hours. The field rotates only expired daily cards automatically. Snapshots keep stale/expired labels visible, and an expired intraday stock residual decays back to the Tickerbot daily baseline.
- **Evaluates the shared context once per instrument.** `GoodIntelligenceMarketContext.evaluate` and `CharactersBeat.motion` run on the server; the stored observation is the exact `market`/`motion` shape the browser used to compute for itself, so every client renders the same directive.
- **Stores one validated row per interval/symbol** in `orb2_field_observations` (migration `0009_orb2_server_field.sql`), with `orb2_field_runs` for the day's counters and `orb2_field_control` for warm markers. Stable-key and warm progress cursors share `orb2_field_queue` under a distinct `cursor:` lane; claims, pending checks, and queue-depth readings filter them out, so no migration is needed. Writes are validated on the way in and again on the way out.

The client is render-only. It polls `GET /api/good-intelligence/v1/orb2/field` every 20 seconds, merges the stored rows into the same observation structures the canvas already drew, and never sends a provider parameter. One read serves every Disc: the snapshot carries each row's own `discId` and a per-Disc `status.discs[]` block, which is what the constellation orbs report. While the dial is intraday, a second `1d` read keeps Discs 2–5's stored daily field in the same snapshot; the page still makes no per-Disc fan-out of requests and no provider call. `Beat now · server tick` becomes a deliberate `POST {action:"tick"}` (an in-isolate 20-second cooldown keeps a page from turning it into a poll), `Refresh focused · baseline` uses the priority lane, and `Recalculate focused · 15m` queues at most 12 reviewed stocks. `Calibrate first deck` queues the explicit 156-call all-scale calibration, which the Cron worker drains one job per minute; automatic cold warm-up persists its progress without a queue burst. The Random US ticker control only selects another member of the reviewed 52-card deck and never fabricates data. The 52-asset firing order renders as a collapsed summary with the next stored symbol, stale/expired card labels remain visible, and hiding the tab pauses nothing server-side: the cron beat runs 24/7/365, and returning to the tab only triggers an immediate re-read. The 60-second provider warm-up and the 12-second client pacing interval are removed, because provider pacing is the Worker's job now.

### Daily baseline × 15-minute residual

The first deck is one reviewed 52-symbol roster, not a second set of cards. Tickerbot `1d` observations establish the stock daily regime. Tickerbot `15m` observations are a bounded, closed-bar residual used only when fresh; this internal Orb 2 scale does not widen the Characters selector or endpoint contract. The composer never averages dollar prices across bar widths: each timeframe is placed inside its own log-space Fibonacci band, and unitful volatility is normalized by `sqrt(26)` because one U.S. regular-trading-hours day contains 26 fifteen-minute bars. VFI and WaveTrend are converted to bounded, dimensionless separation values before combination.

The daily score has 72% weight and the fresh intraday score 28%. A force multiplier can reward fresh agreement or attenuate disagreement, but is capped at `1.35×`; a missing or stale 15m packet returns the daily baseline rather than a neutral guess. The snapshot recomposes against the current clock, so a previously stored intraday packet cannot remain amplified after its freshness window. These fields are display-only: `baselinePosition`, `intradayPosition`, `composedPosition`, `residual`, `freshness`, `forceMultiplier`, `force`, and the scale-normalization metadata do not authorize, transact, or predict.

### Beat types and the dial

The field publishes five beat types. `observation` is the only live provider beat; `message`, `trade`, `authorization`, and `permission` are deterministic local scenarios choreographed from a stored beat by `js/good-intelligence-orb2-scenarios.js`. The dial selects which types a client requests and views; the ticker focus narrows the request and the rendered berths to up to 12 reviewed symbols. Each scenario states its boundary in the UI: no OCM message is transmitted, nothing is bought or settled, nothing is authorized, and no permission is granted. A berth that cannot supply what a type needs (no market summary, no supplied volume, no confirmed close, no motion directive) is refused with a reason rather than animated plausibly.

The Characters Worker deliberately refuses provider work when a durable D1 allowance cannot be checked:

- Massive request admission still requires the D1 telemetry ledger (`COMMUNITY_DB`, migration `0007_massive_daily_call_ledger.sql`) and the shared five-per-minute per-asset-class provider gate; the historical 300/day counter is telemetry-only, not a daily refusal threshold.
- Kraken public-call pacing/daily application guard: `COMMUNITY_DB` and migration `0012_kraken_public_crypto.sql`.
- Tickerbot monthly guard: `COMMUNITY_DB` and migration `0005_tickerbot_call_ledger.sql`. Alpha migration `0006` is historical only; no new Alpha call is made.

The repository's production Wrangler config declares `COMMUNITY_DB` for `community_db`, but branch previews explicitly set `previews.d1_databases` to an empty list. A preview Worker therefore has no D1 binding by design and will refuse due provider requests (and cannot persist Orb 2 history). For production, verify that the deployed Worker still has the binding and that migrations 0005–0012 were applied to that exact database; migration 0010 stores the Kraken crypto ranking, migration 0011 stores verified active futures contracts and lookup cooldowns, and migration 0012 supplies the Kraken call/cache tables. The checked-in config and migration files alone do not prove remote deployment state. Tickerbot can work while another provider's independent ledger or cache table is missing. The Orb 2 resolver discovers no more than one uncached commodity product per tick; scheduled work is still capped at one work unit per Cron invocation, while FX/Commodities/Indices retain Massive's five-per-minute per-class provider gate and a telemetry-only historical daily count. Crypto uses Kraken's separate public-call gate. Verified commodity tickers retain their contract provenance in the stored observation. Waiting alone does not resolve a missing configuration/migration; no upstream request is sent for the refused berth.

## Server-side beat history

`worker/good-intelligence-orb2-beats.mjs` stores compact, validated market observations and the corresponding Characters motion directive in `orb2_saved_beats`. The browser keeps only a random opaque session UUID plus a best-effort local recovery copy. The server keeps at most 120 beats per browser session for 90 days. It stores no API keys, identity records, or full bar series. Synthetic rehearsal is not written as live server history.

**Live-only view.** Orb 2's browser view no longer offers a synthetic/demo data source. A failed, unconfigured, or refused live beat leaves the affected berths held with an explicit refusal message — the model keeps its last reviewed observation and nothing synthetic is substituted. Old local recovery copies that still contain clearly labeled rehearsal events remain honestly labeled and are never re-saved as live history.

Apply migration `0008_orb2_saved_beats.sql` to the same D1 database used by the Worker before enabling server persistence. If the history table is missing or D1 is unavailable, the page labels the condition and preserves the local recovery copy when browser storage is available. This persistence route never initiates a provider request.

## Ticker-orb mode and Center Origin

- When Good Intelligence mounts Orb 2 over its reviewed watchlist, the toolbar offers a `TIMEFRAME` selector; switching it rebuilds the ticker orbs locally without another network request. Observed orbs float from pure packet metrics rather than sector slots, and the empty state names the reviewed watchlist it is waiting on.
- Center Origin carries the parts the author specified on the whiteboard: an **inner shell** inside the model, the **declared PDH phase** as a rotation shift of that whole assembly, and the origin drawn as the **CPU node** that the **Human Key** secures. Phase, gate state, and the UTS clock are read from the local kernel snapshot (`HOS.observe()`) and the local UTS math, then shown on the canvas in one `ORIGIN · CPU — 0•000•000•0000 · GATE … · PDH … · UTS …` strip. An unread kernel is reported as `GATE UNREAD` / `PDH UNDECLARED`; nothing is defaulted, and no gate state authorizes anything.
- **No fractal, flame, or film engine is attached to this view.** The `OrbFilm` surface film, the `Demo.flame` IFS core, the `.flame` reference-triangle billboard, and the `Fractal core` toggle were removed at the author's instruction: the fractal engines added a second, unrelated animation system on top of market geometry. The Orb app, the Fractal Flame app, and the OCM visualization keep their own flame/film rendering unchanged.
- Two bordered status boxes are deliberately absent from the canvas overlay so the geometry stays unobstructed; what remains is the volume-flow readout, the Center Origin strip, and the gesture hint.

## Fibonacci-band metrics

- `GoodIntelligenceOrb2.fibonacciBandMetrics(market)` is the shared, pure definition of a ticker's position inside its own log-space Fibonacci Bollinger band: `p = 0.5 + ((ln(close) - ln(basis)) / (close >= basis ? ln(upper) - ln(basis) : ln(basis) - ln(lower))) / 2`, so multiplicatively equal upper/lower excursions read the same distance from neutral. Volatility uses `(ln(upper) - ln(lower)) × 100`, normalized stretch from the midline is potential energy, and a supplied band break names its side. Nonpositive inputs remain held because they are outside the log domain.
- Incomplete or invalid packets stay `held` with `p = null` — unknown, never neutral. `CharactersBeat.gaugeOf()` uses `ln(close) - ln(basis)` for its FBB bias, and the NAB composite's `NabComposites.displacement()` applies the same normalized log-space distance. The focused Orb 2, Characters, and NAB tests pin those consumers.
- The double-helix arrangement that once consumed it in Orb 2 (Series A/B strands, rungs, eased berths) is retired along with the fractal pass. The metric now contributes to the free-floating market-data placement because it is market arithmetic, not decoration; `tests/orb2-baseline-test.mjs` pins it.

## Surfaces: what is whose

| Surface | What it renders | Where |

## Validation

- `npm run test:characters` covers the provider contracts and beat/motion engine.
- `node --test tests/good-intelligence-orb2-test.mjs tests/good-intelligence-orb2-worker-test.mjs` covers the Orb 2 render-only field, the beat-type dial and ticker focus, the manual server controls, warm-up behavior on the server field, beat persistence, the storage boundary, the shared-berth separation (crowded berths open to the floor, the first node keeps its packet berth, radii and held placeholders are untouched, and the resolver is deterministic, idempotent, and leaves clear fields undisturbed), and the constellation (the berth is a pure function of catalog order, the strip carries one chip per Disc with its own stored status, an empty Disc is labeled instead of filled, and a step, click, slide, or arrow-key move walks the ring without a provider call).
