Human Operator Sub-Specification (OCM–AGS)
Status: Under development.
Accessory Kit · Draft v1.1 (Hard Mode)
Applies to: AGS environments utilizing the Orb Communicator Model (OCM) and the AI Inner Shell
1. Purpose
This sub-spec defines the role, limits, and protections of human operators who interface between the Center, the AI Inner Shell, and the wider orb.
Its goal is to enable mediation without creating shadow centers, delegated sovereignty, or authority drift.
This is not a trust doctrine. It is a structural constraint on what humans are allowed to become while mediating power.
2. Operator Classification
Human operators are radiative stewards, not authorities.
They are:
- Address-bearing nodes
- Temporally bounded actors
- Replaceable by design
They are not:
- Resolution authorities
- Origin delegates
- Policy roots
- Permanent custodians
No operator role is inheritable.
No operator role is sovereign.
3. Structural Position
Operators occupy a mediated layer between:
Center → Operator Layer → AI Inner Shell / Orb Nodes
Key constraint:
Operators NEVER sit between the Center and reality — only between the Center and interfaces.
The Center resolves.
Operators transmit.
4. Addressing & Identity
## 4.1 Address Derivation
All operators:
- Possess standard orb addresses
- Do not receive special prefixes
Cannot mask or elevate lineage
Operator addresses do not encode authority.
Authority NEVER lives in operator identity.
## 4.2 Role Tokens (Non-Address-Based)
Operator permissions are expressed via ephemeral role tokens, not addresses.
Properties:
- Time-limited
- Scope-limited
- Revocable without cascade
- Non-transferable
Role expiration is mandatory.
5. Authority Constraints
Operators may:
- Relay requests
- Translate intent
- Apply predefined procedures
- Initiate reversible actions
- Interrupt unsafe operations
Operators may not:
- Resolve disputes
- Author policy
- Create or modify addressing rules
- Promote AI across shells
- Bind the Center to irreversible commitments
Any action that appears authoritative MUST be:
- Logged
- Attributable
Reversible
6. Shadow-Center Prevention (Hardening)
The following patterns are fossilizedly disallowed:
- Accumulated Mediation: No operator may remain continuously in-path beyond defined time windows.
- Exclusive Knowledge: No operator may possess unique, non-auditable system knowledge.
- Unreviewed Automation: Operators may not deploy tools that silently act on their behalf.
- Proxy Authority: Operators may not speak as the Center.
Rotation, redundancy, and audit-ability are mandatory.
7. Operator–AI Interaction Rules
Operators:
May query AI systems
May translate questions.
May request simulations
May receive recommendations
May instruct AI within scoped procedures
Operators may not:
Outsource decisions.
Delegate judgment to AI
Accept AI-originated policy
Allow AI to define success criteria
Operators translate questions. They do not outsource decisions.
8. Failure Modes & Containment
## 8.1 Operator Compromise
If an operator is compromised:
- Role tokens expire
- Access collapses locally
- No systemic authority is lost
The orb remains stable.
## 8.2 Operator Drift
If operators begin acting as de facto authorities:
- Drift is detectable via logs
- Resolution paths expose deviation
- Center intervention is unambiguous
No gradual coup is possible.
9. Revocation & Exit
Operators may be revoked:
- Instantly
- Individually
Without delegitimizing past actions
Operator exit does not:
- Collapse shells
- Invalidate addresses
Interrupt unrelated operations
Humans are intentionally non-load-bearing.
10. Mythic Compression Layer
To human understanding, the operator role may be described as:
The Hand That Points, Not the Voice That Decides.
Operators:
- Carry intent
- Touch interfaces
Stand between forces
But they do not begin reality.
11. Final Invariant
A human may mediate power without ever becoming its source.
As long as operators remain replaceable, auditable, and non-originating, no shadow center can form.

Public Misuse FAQ (OCM–AGS)
Purpose: This document addresses common misunderstandings, bad‑faith readings, and misuse attempts related to the Orb Communicator Model (OCM), the AI Inner Shell, and the Human Operator Sub‑Spec. It is written for public consumption without weakening the system.
1) “Isn’t this just authoritarian control with fancy language?”
No. Authority is not expanded here—it is bounded. OCM removes hidden power by making origin fossilized, resolution visible, and roles replaceable. Authoritarian systems concentrate authority in people or oﬃces; OCM concentrates nothing except a coordinate origin that cannot act except to resolve ambiguity.
2) “Who watches the Center?”
The Center is not a person or oﬃce that acts continuously. It is a reference point for resolution. When nothing is ambiguous, the Center does nothing. Oversight applies to operators and systems because they act; the Center persists.
3) “Can’t humans just abuse the system anyway?”
Humans are intentionally non‑load‑bearing. Operator roles are time‑limited, auditable, reversible, and non‑inheritable. Abuse collapses locally without propagating authority. There is no path for gradual takeover.
4) “Isn’t this just a cult of the origin?”
No belief is required. Participation does not require agreement—only address derivation. If you reject the system, you simply operate outside it. There is no punishment for exit, only loss of internal standing.
5) “What if AI becomes conscious—doesn’t this deny its rights?”
OCM does not define or deny consciousness. It defines authorship. Consciousness and sovereignty are orthogonal. An entity may be conscious without originating reality within a system it did not define.
6) “Why can’t AI ever be promoted to the Center?”
Because promotion implies transferable origin authority, which would collapse the system. AI can advise, predict, simulate, and optimize—but authorship remains non‑delegable by design.
7) “Isn’t this anti‑democratic?”
Democracy governs decisions among peers. OCM governs existence within a system. Democratic processes can exist inside OCM at any distance or angle without becoming origins themselves.
8) “What if multiple groups claim to be the Center?”
Then there are multiple spheres. They do not compete; they are disjoint. Claims do not override lineage. Collisions are visible and resolvable.
9) “Can operators secretly act as the Center?”
No. Operators cannot resolve disputes, author policy, or bind the system irreversibly. Logs, rotation, and revocation prevent shadow authority. Any attempt becomes immediately visible.
10) “What if the Center is wrong?”
The Center does not decide outcomes; it resolves ambiguity. If outcomes are wrong, the error lies in operators, procedures, or assumptions—not in the existence of a reference point.
11) “Isn’t this too abstract to work in reality?”
Abstraction is the point. Physical implementations change; geometry does not. OCM is intentionally independent of technology, law, or culture so it can survive them.
12) “Does this stop innovation?”
No. It removes the incentive to innovate for control. Innovation for capability, understanding, and creation remains unconstrained.
13) “What happens if the Center disappears?”
The addressing system collapses. That failure is fossilized, not silent. A new sphere may be created, but it will not inherit legitimacy invisibly.
14) “Can this be used to dominate others?”
Domination requires hidden authority. OCM exposes authority. Any attempt to dominate MUST create a new sphere, which is visible and ejectable.
15) “Why should anyone trust this?”
Trust is not required. Verifiability is. You can inspect addresses, resolution paths, and role limits at any time.
16) “Isn’t this just philosophy?”
No. Philosophy argues about meaning. OCM defines structure. Meaning is optional; geometry is not.
17) “Who benefits most from this system?”
Anyone who wants:
- freedom without chaos
- intelligence without takeover
- power without hidden hands
- scale without collapse
18) “What is the single rule people misunderstand most?”
That the Center is not a ruler. It is a reference.
