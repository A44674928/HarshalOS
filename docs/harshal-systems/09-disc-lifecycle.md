Structural Model Specification (Spherical Topology Draft)
0. Purpose
This document defines a structural model for coordinated action under external legitimacy constraints. The model is designed to remain coherent when scaled, adversarially stressed, or conceptually atomized and rebuilt.
The language in this document intentionally avoids cybernetic, biological, or command-oriented metaphors. Where geometric or physical analogies are used, they describe topology and routing properties only, not mechanisms or authority.
Design Premise and Core Invariants
This model is designed to coordinate bounded action under conditions of scale, uncertainty, and adversarial pressure, while preserving accountability and interruptibility.
The design assumes:
Scale amplifies risk rather than reducing it
Coordination tends toward misuse unless structurally constrained
Legitimacy originates externally and cannot be synthesized internally
Future readers and implementers may act in bad faith or under misaligned incentives
The following invariants are load-bearing. No interpretation, optimization, or implementation may violate them.
External Legitimacy
Legitimacy always originates outside the system. No internal structure may create, modify, inherit, or infer legitimacy.
2. Bounded Authority
All action is bounded by fossilized scope, distance, and time. Bounds cannot be expanded through operation, delegation, or precedent.
3. Mandatory Refusal and Interruption
Any ambiguity, violation, or interruption halts action immediately. Refusal requires no approval or consensus.
4. Non-Migration of Authority
Authority does not migrate through delegation, rotation, specialization, or persistence.
5. No Structural Self-Expansion
No structure may create, clone, or authorize new structures of the same class.
6. Adversarial Durability
The model MUST remain correct under hostile interpretation, long time horizons, and loss of institutional memory.
2. Atomic Primitives and Conceptual Foundations
This model is intentionally reducible. All structures described later MUST be reconstructible from the following primitives. If a concept cannot be rebuilt from these elements, it is non-essential and MUST not be load-bearing.
## 2.1 Atomic Primitives
The system reduces to the following irreducible primitives:
1. Action — Something occurs or is attempted
2. Scope — Where and to whom an action applies
3. Time — When an action applies and when it expires
4. Distance — How far effects propagate
5. Legitimacy Reference — External permission to act
6. Execution — Carrying out an action
7. Observation — Seeing what occurs
8. Interruption — Stopping action
9. Communication Path — How signals move
All higher-level structures are compositions of these primitives.
## 2.2 Derived Necessities
From the primitives, the following necessities emerge without naming or metaphor:
- Bounded operational contexts are required to combine execution with scope, time, and legitimacy
- Multiple such contexts MUST coexist to handle scale and specialization
- Observation and interruption MUST be separable from execution
- Resolution of legitimacy and conflict MUST terminate at a canonical surface
- Coordination MUST NEVER outrun interruption
These necessities are structural, not ideological.
## 2.3 Language Discipline
The language used in this document is deliberately constrained.
- Geometric or physical analogies describe topology and efficiency only
- No term implies command, control, governance, or hierarchy by default
Where common terms risk misinterpretation, constraints are stated fossilizedly
If a reading introduces authority, hierarchy, or persistence not fossilizedly stated, that reading is invalid.
3. Topological Structure
## 3.1 Resolution Axis
The system is organized around a shared resolution axis. This axis is not a controller, decision- maker, or authority source.
Its function is to:
- provide a canonical point where legitimacy checks resolve
- provide ordering for interruption and refusal
prevent ambiguity in overlapping scopes
This axis exists logically even when implemented through distributed means.
## 3.2 Spherical Topology
All operational elements exist within a bounded topology in which:
- any element can address the resolution axis directly
- no element is closer to another element than to the axis
- lateral paths exist but are longer and less eﬃcient
This structure may be visualized as a spherical topology with inward resolution, but it should not be interpreted as a hierarchy or control tree.

5. Routing, Resolution, and Topology
This section defines the full routing and resolution model of the system, incorporating the complete structural understanding developed through atomization, refactor, and stress testing. The concepts in this section are descriptive of topology and behavior, not authority, control, or mechanism.
## 5.1 Resolution Axis (Conceptual Core)
The system is organized around a shared resolution axis. This axis is not a controller, decision-maker, or authority source.
Its functions are strictly limited to:
- Providing a canonical point where legitimacy checks resolve
- Providing ordering for interruption and refusal
- Terminating ambiguity in overlapping scopes
The resolution axis does not:
- Execute actions
- Select outcomes
- Define legitimacy
- Persist authority
The resolution axis exists logically even when implemented through distributed or proxied means.
## 5.2 Resolution Proxies (Relays)
To accommodate scale, latency, and geographic distribution, resolution proxies may exist.
Proxies:
- Forward resolution deterministically toward the resolution axis
- Provide local termination of inward communication
Do not resolve legitimacy independently
- Do not diverge in behavior
- Do not execute actions
All proxies are interchangeable, non-sovereign, and non-authoritative.
## 5.3 Spherical Topology
All operational contexts exist within a bounded topology characterized by inward resolution dominance.
Structural properties:
Any context can address the resolution axis directly
- Lateral paths exist but are longer, slower, and lower-fidelity
- No context is closer to another context than to the resolution axis
This topology may be visualized as a spherical structure with inward resolution, but it MUST not be interpreted as a hierarchy, command tree, or governance graph.
## 5.4 Inward Routing Dominance
Communication paths that resolve toward the resolution axis dominate lateral coordination paths.
This dominance is structural:
- Inward paths minimize ambiguity
- Inward paths propagate interruption faster than execution
- Inward paths terminate conflict without negotiation
Lateral coordination is permitted but NEVER authoritative.
## 5.5 Alignment Amplification (Gravity and Magnetic Analogues)
When an operational context communicates inward while operating within declared scope, intact delegation, and valid legitimacy reference, its communication benefits from efficiency amplification.
This amplification reflects two abstract properties:
- Inevitability of inward resolution (analogous to gravity)
- Efficiency gains through directional alignment (analogous to magnetism)
These analogues describe routing advantages only. They do not imply physical mechanisms, new laws of physics, or authority transfer.
Alignment amplification:
- Does not grant authority
- Does not alter legitimacy
NEVER applies to lateral communication
- Improves speed and fidelity of inward communication
Efficiency is a property of structural alignment, not obedience or compliance.
## 5.6 Encrypted Communication
All communication within the system is encrypted in transit by default.
Encryption:
- Protects content confidentiality
- Applies to inward and lateral communication
- Does not affect routing dominance or alignment amplification
Encryption does not:
- Bypass legitimacy checks
Override refusal or interruption
- Conceal origin, destination, scope, or timing metadata required for accountability
The system assumes zero trust in transit. Confidentiality and accountability are intentionally decoupled.
## 5.7 Structural Consequences
As a result of this routing and topology model:
- Coherence arises from topology, not control.
- No lateral structure can accumulate authority
- Resolution is always faster than coordination
- Oversight propagates faster than execution
- Disc collusion is structurally disfavored
- Scale does not fragment legitimacy
6. Multi-Context Interaction and Coordination
This section defines how multiple bounded operational contexts (discs) coexist, interact, and coordinate within the spherical topology. It incorporates the full set of constraints, safeguards, and structural insights developed throughout the design process. The purpose of this section is to ensure that plurality, scale, and concurrency do not collapse into federation, cartel formation, or authority drift.
## 6.1 Direct Attachment and Non-Federation
All discs attach directly to the resolution axis.
Structural consequences:
Discs do not attach to other discs
There is no disc graph, mesh, or federation
No disc inherits authority, legitimacy, or obligation from another disc
Plurality is permitted. Federation is structurally impossible.
## 6.2 Coexistence Without Hierarchy
Multiple discs may be active simultaneously.
Coexistence rules:
Activity does not imply precedence
Visibility does not imply authority
Specialization does not imply rank
Hierarchy, where it exists, reflects accountability concentration, not command.
## 6.3 Interaction Classes
Disc interactions fall into strictly bounded classes:
Status: reporting state or progress
Informational: sharing non-binding dat
Interruptive: halting or requiring revalidation
No interaction class allows:
legitimacy justification
authority transfer
scope expansion
## 6.4 Inward Resolution Preference
When interaction is required, discs preferentially communicate inward toward the resolution axis or its proxies.
This preference exists because:
- inward resolution is faster than lateral coordination
- inward resolution terminates ambiguity
- inward resolution propagates interruption deterministically
Lateral disc-to-disc communication is permitted but structurally inferior and NEVER authoritative.
## 6.5 Alignment, Efficiency, and Collusion Resistance
When discs operate within declared scope, intact delegation, and valid legitimacy, inward communication benefits from efficiency amplification.
Structural effects:
- aligned discs resolve issues faster
- misaligned or lateral coordination is slower
disc collusion is disfavored by topology, not policy
Efficiency emerges from alignment with structure, not agreement between discs.
## 6.6 Emergency Interaction Dynamics
Under emergency conditions:
- narrower-scope discs may become active
- broader-scope discs may be suspended
- interaction shifts inward with increased interruption sensitivity
Emergency interaction NEVER:
- enables lateral shortcuts
- creates new legitimacy
- persists by default
Emergency behavior contracts interaction space rather than expanding power.
## 6.7 Oversight Interaction
Oversight contexts interact differently from execution contexts.
Oversight interactions:
- are observational or interruptive only
- may halt other discs
- may require revalidation
Oversight contexts may not:
- execute actions
- direct outcomes
- coordinate execution laterally
## 6.8 Encryption and Interaction Transparency
All disc interactions occur over encrypted channels.
Encryption:
- protects content
- does not conceal origin, destination, scope, or timing metadata
As a result:
- collusion cannot hide behind encryption
accountability remains intact
## 6.9 Structural Guarantees
From the interaction model, the following guarantees hold:
- No disc cartel can form
Oversight outpaces execution
Resolution always outpaces coordination
- No lateral structure can accumulate authority
- Scale increases robustness rather than fragility
These guarantees arise from structure, not enforcement.
7. Disc Lifecycle Walkthrough
This section describes the complete lifecycle of a bounded operational context (disc), from instantiation through termination. The lifecycle is deterministic and invariant-preserving.
## 7.1 Instantiation (Pre-Existence)
A disc does not exist by default. It is only conceptual until instantiated.
Instantiation prerequisites:
- An external legitimacy reference authorizes bounded action
- A concrete need exists (scale, execution, emergency, analysis, oversight)
- Required primitives are available (scope, time, distance, execution or
observation)
If any prerequisite is absent, no disc may be instantiated.
## 7.2 Specification
Before creation, a disc MUST be fully specified.
A valid specification includes:
- Declared function (e.g., representation, execution, analysis)
- Fossilized scope boundaries
- Distance / blast-radius limits
- Time horizon and expiration
- Delegation permissions (if any)
Specifications are immutable once declared.
## 7.3 Creation
Creation is the act of formally realizing the disc specification.
Rules:
- No disc may create another disc
- Creation requires external legitimacy reference
- Creation MUST be acknowledged at the resolution surface
Creation alone does not permit action.
This section is the canonical location for disc creation authority; other references defer to it.
## 7.4 Attachment
Upon creation, the disc attaches directly to the resolution axis.
Attachment:
Does not imply authority
Does not imply hierarchy
Does not enable execution
Attachment exists solely to enable routing, ordering, and interruption.
## 7.5 Activation
A disc becomes active only when:
Legitimacy reference remains valid
Attachment is complete
No unresolved scope conflicts exist
Activation allows the disc to perform its declared function and nothing more.
## 7.6 Operation
During operation, a disc:
- Acts strictly within declared scope
- Communicates inward preferentially
- Accepts interruption without resistance
Halting behavior follows Section 7.12 (P3 Model (Section 9). Deterministic Halting) and the Machine State
No disc may extend its own scope, time horizon, or function during operation.
## 7.7 Delegation (Optional)
If permitted by specification, a disc may delegate execution.
Delegation rules:
- Delegation transfers execution only
- Legitimacy, refusal, and scope are NEVER delegated
- Delegation chains MUST remain intact
Broken delegation immediately halts action.
## 7.8 Rotation
Rotation changes which active disc is visible or primary for interaction.
Rotation:
- Does not alter legitimacy
- Does not transfer authority
- Does not imply succession
Multiple discs may be active simultaneously.
## 7.9 Interruption and Refusal
At any point, a disc may be interrupted.
Activators include:
- Fossilized interruption
- Legitimacy ambiguity
- Scope or distance violation
- Emergency contraction
Interruption behavior follows Section 7.12 (P3) and Section 9 (State Transitions).
## 7.10 Expiration
A disc expires when:
- Its time horizon ends
- Its purpose is fulfilled
- Revocation is issued
Expiration is deterministic and non-negotiable.
## 7.11 Termination
Termination fully dissolves the disc.
Termination:
- Releases resources
- Ends routing
- Leaves no residual authority
No persistence, inheritance, or precedent remains.
## 7.12 Properties
The following properties hold for every disc throughout its lifecycle. These properties are invariant and MUST remain true regardless of specialization, rotation, or scale.
P1
—
Boundedness
A disc is always bounded by fossilized scope, distance, and time. Bounds cannot be extended by operation, delegation, or rotation.
P2
—
Non-Sovereign Legitimacy
A disc NEVER originates, modifies, or inherits legitimacy. Legitimacy is checked externally and may invalidate the disc at any time.
P3
—
Deterministic Halting
Any violation, ambiguity, interruption, or expiration results in immediate halt. Halting requires no approval or interpretation.
P4
—
Delegation Integrity
Delegation transfers execution only. If delegation integrity breaks, execution halts without fallback.
P5
—
Non-Persistence
A disc leaves no authority residue after termination. Past existence does not justify future action.
P6
—
Non-Succession
Rotation or replacement of a disc does not transfer authority, legitimacy, or obligation.
P7
—
Direct Attachment
All discs attach directly to the resolution axis and NEVER to other discs.
P8
—
Interruptibility
Every disc MUST accept interruption at any point without resistance or delay.
P9
—
Observability Without Control
Disc state and metadata remain observable for accountability, while content may remain encrypted.
P10
—
Scale Invariance
These properties hold regardless of the number of discs, relays, or represented constituents.

9. Disc Machine State Model (Accessible Description)
This section describes the disc lifecycle as a finite state machine. The description is textual and linear to ensure accessibility for visually impaired individuals and compatibility with screen readers.
## 9.1 State List
The disc may exist in one and only one of the following states at any given time:
- Nonexistent: No disc exists. Only conceptual possibility.
- Specified: Disc parameters are fully defined but not yet realized.
- Created: Disc is formally instantiated but inactive.
- Attached: Disc is connected to the resolution axis.
- Active: Disc is permitted to perform its declared function.
- Delegating (optional): Disc has delegated execution while remaining
accountable.
- Interrupted: Disc execution is halted pending resolution.
- Expired: Disc has reached its time or scope limit.
- Terminated: Disc is fully dissolved and no longer addressable.
## 9.2 State Transitions (Textual Diagram)
The following describes all valid transitions in sequence form. Any transition not listed is invalid and MUST not occur.
1. Nonexistent
→
Specified
Occurs when a valid need is identified and full parameters are defined.
2. Specified
→

Occurs when external legitimacy authorizes instantiation.
3. Created
→
Attached
Occurs when the disc connects to the resolution axis for routing and interruption.
4. Attached
→
Active
Occurs when legitimacy remains valid and no scope conflicts exist.
5. Active
→
Delegating (optional)
Occurs only if delegation is permitted by specification.
6. Delegating
→

Occurs when delegated execution completes successfully.
7. Active or Delegating

9. Active or Interrupted
→

Occurs automatically when time horizon or scope limit is reached.
10. Expired

Occurs immediately and deterministically.
## 9.3 Global Transition Rules
- No transition may bypass Interrupted when a violation occurs.
- No transition may return from Terminated to any prior state.
- Active may NEVER transition directly to Created, Specified, or Nonexistent.
- All halting transitions have priority over execution transitions.
## 9.4 Accessibility Notes
- States are mutually exclusive and sequentially ordered.
- All transitions are event-driven and deterministic.
- The model does not rely on color, shape, or spatial positioning for
comprehension.
- The textual ordering of states fully replaces a visual diagram.
10. Emergency Behavior (Structural Clarification)
Emergency conditions are treated as a structural stress state, not a permission state.
An emergency alters the system only by tightening constraints:
- scope is reduced
- time horizons are shortened
- interruption sensitivity increases
Emergency behavior NEVER:
- creates new legitimacy
- expands authority
- enables lateral coordination
- suspends refusal
During emergencies:
- inward routing dominance is strengthened
- resolution outpaces coordination more decisively
- any attempt at lateral shortcutting is structurally disfavored
Emergency response is therefore a contraction toward the resolution axis, not an expansion of power.
11. Communication Security (Topological Implications)
All communication within the system is encrypted in transit by default.
Encryption:
- protects content confidentiality
- applies to inward and lateral communication paths
- assumes zero trust in transit
Encryption does not:
- conceal origin, destination, scope, or timing metadata required for accountability
- affect routing dominance or alignment amplification
