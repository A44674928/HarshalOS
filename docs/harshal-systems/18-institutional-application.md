Abstract
A specific, concrete human–machine hybrid system is disclosed in which legitimate operation is structurally dependent on an accountability binding anchor to a specific human key. The system employs externally generated sensory-state signaling implemented by physical signal generators, accountability-gated control logic executed by one or more processors, immutable logging, defined non-operational failure modes, and inert addressable carrier units. Technical Field. Certain embodiments are applicable to supervised medical, laboratory, and safety-critical environments.

Existing approaches often rely on autonomous system behavior or invasive biological intervention, which can introduce unacceptable risk, diffusion of responsibility, and regulatory uncertainty. When escalation conditions are satisfied, the control logic transitions the system into an autonomous medical authority escalation mode permitting initiation of medical actions without prior human approval of the exact action taken. Autonomous authority is bounded by predefined safety constraints and is automatically terminated upon restoration of viable human oversight. In one exemplary embodiment, upon detection of acute respiratory compromise and loss of external oversight, the system autonomously initiates an authorized respiratory support action. In another exemplary embodiment, upon detection of circulatory instability and loss of communication, the system autonomously initiates a stabilization protocol selected from a predefined set of medically appropriate actions. In all cases, immutable audit logs are preserved.
