## 2. The bundled business model

**FRONTIERSHIP** is the company/master brand; **OUTPOST ONE** is the flagship property; **Safety First** is the operating standard. This is an income-first facility-and-business concept: the residence, lab/program, operating entity, and room-month economics belong in one coordinated plan. Later sites may use lower-cost models; they are not a second current design option for OUTPOST ONE. The owner’s pricing position is San Francisco-level bundle parity plus amenity over-match, but local demand/comparables have not been validated. The resident offer is priced per rentable room-month and combines accommodation with lab/program access and a defined amenity roster. Do not split the same bundle into separate lodging and program revenue unless the contract and accounting model explicitly do so.

The prior narrative and spreadsheet both used $4,800, but the old workbook calls its units **57 monetized beds**, while the current owner direction defines **57 rentable bedrooms/rooms**. The selected price now resolves the amount and pricing concept; the workbook still has an obsolete capacity denominator and five-owner-bed allocation. Its ≈1/12 owner-share premise and the separate “2/5 of The Owner’s Area” note are also unreconciled with two owner bedrooms plus three unallocated extras. It is not the current room schedule or a lean-project pro forma. A new model must use room-months, occupied room-months, included service costs, and any sponsor/customer payment separately.

## 3. Lean repeatable design basis

Use a single coordinated, repeatable layout across the whole program rather than carrying forward the cost-unlimited 40,000-square-foot/max-capacity premise. Keep the design income-first, preserve modern forest-cabin character, and minimize disturbance; optimize all-in cost per rentable room **without** pretending the current cost is known.

1. **Repeat rental rooms:** one or a small number of standard room modules; align structure, doors/windows, and accessible circulation. Do not publish a bed/person count until a life-safety schedule establishes it.
2. **Consolidate services:** test stacked wet walls and shared bath/laundry/kitchen/commons to reduce duplicated plumbing and service area, subject to the adopted code, accessibility, privacy, and operations model.
3. **Right-size the common core:** keep the resident bundle coherent; define the lab/demo, medical-capability, dining, wellness, and greenhouse scopes before pricing or drawing them as built spaces.
4. **Bermed wing:** retain as a concept to test against grading, structure, energy, drainage, egress, and cost. Do not assume berming is free or remove it without a documented comparison.
5. **Frontier without colo capex:** BYO rented compute + demo closet supports the 20–50 kW sustained capability goal. Do not import the legacy owned GPU nodes, full BSL-2 wet lab, PV/BESS/generator plant, or 40+ kW colo package into the baseline unless separately reselected and funded.
6. **No quantified savings yet:** share/bunk layouts can change cost per bedspace but do not automatically lower cost per room, operating cost, or approved capacity. No savings are booked until measured test-fits and costs exist.

**Cost-per-room definition:** for the lean plan, track `(all-in project capex ÷ 57 intended rentable rooms)` and separately `(annual operating cost ÷ occupied room-months)`. The old $19M–$24M / $21.5M max-house estimate divided by 57 is only a historical allocation ($333K–$421K, $377K mid); it is **not** the lean design's cost per room. Current lean capex, OPEX, and capital ask remain TBD.

## 4. Investor framing and stage gates

Present this as an early operating-company plus facility concept, not a stabilized real-estate return. The investor thesis is that a $4,800 all-in room-month can support a safety-first, frontier-scale AI residency; it requires proof of payer demand, room inventory, the included service package, lean build cost, and operating margin.

- **Stage 0 — current, no-spend:** freeze the room/product definitions; prepare a lean test-fit brief, bundle-cost ledger, risks, and investor/customer evidence plan. No paid diligence is authorized by this document.
- **Stage 1 — capitalized diligence:** only after a funding/partner decision, obtain the needed site/utility/use evidence and professional test-fit/costing. Do not infer permission, service availability, or room capacity.
- **Stage 2 — funded delivery:** set a construction/operating phase only after room schedule, bundle, demand, capital stack, and safety/site gates are documented. No phase size or opening date is selected.
