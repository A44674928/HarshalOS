## The concept

**FRONTIERSHIP** is the master brand; **OUTPOST ONE** is its Safety First high-desert AI-lab HQ. The operating concept combines 30+ day residency (no STR), lab/program access, shared amenities, a frontier-scale AI capability, and a small emergency-capable medical concept. This is one combined facility-and-business concept, not two parallel property plans.

### Selected resident bundle

The current owner-selected rate is **$4,800 for each rentable room-month**, including the room, lab/program access, and defined amenities. The exact amenity roster, service scope, contract, payer, and room-level service capacity remain to be defined and validated. The room-month is the billing unit. Shared occupancy may be considered, but **the $4,800 total rate does not multiply per resident**. Occupant count and legal capacity are not established.

This is a working assumption—not verified market demand. The owner believes investors may be found at this rate, but no market validation, customer commitment, or investor commitment exists. The owner’s pricing stance is San Francisco-level bundle parity plus amenity over-match; no local demand or comparable-rate study has validated that positioning. The rejected $1,100–$1,900/bed-month study tiers remain historical only for this property in 2026. The old $3,600/month whole-furnished-2BR-ADU case is a different, historical product—not the selected OUTPOST ONE rate.

## Room scenario and transparent arithmetic

**62 bedrooms:** 57 intended rentals + 2 owner bedrooms + 3 extras unallocated. These are bedroom counts, not approved beds, bedspaces, people, or guaranteed revenue units. Keep the three extras unassigned until a room schedule and approvals exist.

At 57 rented rooms and full occupancy, gross bundle revenue would be **57 × $4,800 × 12 = $3,283,200 per year**. This is only full-occupancy arithmetic, before vacancy, discounts, service costs, staffing, taxes, reserves, or debt. It is **not** a forecast, NOI, cap rate, or investment return. The legacy workbook applies $4,800 to 57 modeled beds, not the newly selected 57-room basis, and also carries a legacy 88% occupancy, OPEX, and max-house capex scope. Do not use its return calculations as the lean-plan underwriting case.

## Current development direction

The active direction is **one lean, repeatable plan for the whole build, optimizing cost per rentable room**. This replaces the former cost-unlimited max-house as the baseline. Repeat room modules, consolidate shared service cores, and right-size common program spaces without assuming a particular floor plan, occupant load, savings, capex, or opening phase.

- Retain the **bermed-wing** concept only as an option to test against grading, structure, drainage, energy, egress, and cost. Its fit in the lean plan is unresolved.
- Maintain the **20–50 kW sustained capability target** through rented/BYO compute plus a demo closet. This is not a utility commitment, an owned GPU plant, or a colocation campus.
- Preserve the current **18% disturbance envelope, 210 CY grading, and five ponderosa retained** as targets pending survey and measured design.
- Keep wellness, greenhouse, commons, and the small emergency-capable medical function conceptual until scope, amenity inclusion, operating cost, clinical/licensure/staffing requirements, and life safety are defined.
- Lean capex, annual OPEX, cost per room, a capital ask, demand, occupancy, construction phase, and opening date are **TBD**. No numeric savings are claimed.

## Evidence gates before investment underwriting

1. Confirm survey/boundary and legal access; user/site reports are not independently verified.
2. Assess zoning, use, room schedule, accessibility, fire/life safety, and approved occupancy; the overall zoning/code review remains **NOT ASSESSED**.
3. Establish power, water, wastewater, utility and load, and site-system feasibility.
4. Define the room-month contract and all-in bundle, amenity roster, payer, service capacity, sharing rules, costs, and paying demand.
5. Produce a costed lean test-fit and linked operating/capital model; do not reuse max-house economics as if current.
6. Define medical scope and any licensing, staffing, EMS, and treatment boundaries before representing a service.
