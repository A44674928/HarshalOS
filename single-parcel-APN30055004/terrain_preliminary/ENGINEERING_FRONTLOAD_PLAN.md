# FRONTIERSHIP · OUTPOST ONE — Engineering Front-Load Plan

**Prepared:** 2026-10-17
**Workstream:** **ENGINEERED CONNECTIONS · PRE-DESIGN EVIDENCE**
**Purpose:** Reduce avoidable discovery, coordination, and redesign during later licensed-professional work by preparing a traceable, internally coordinated basis package now.
**Boundary:** This plan organizes evidence and preparatory design work; it is not an engineering design, zoning determination, permit set, professional certification, or construction document. Nothing here is sealed or represented as a licensed professional's work.

## The useful distinction

We can build **evidence for design decisions** here: sourced inputs, explicit requirements, calculations with stated assumptions, coordinated test fits, alternatives, clash/issue logs, and a clear handoff. That work can reduce the time a professional spends reconstructing the project brief and reconciling conflicting assumptions.

We cannot create **evidence that a licensed professional has engineered, reviewed, or sealed the work**. Survey control, geotechnical findings, official zoning/use interpretations, engineered calculations, final specifications, permit submissions, and professional sign-off must come from the appropriate qualified sources and professionals. The application's “Engineered Connections” phase is a workflow label; it does not certify engineering.

## Zoning-first concept rules for the next test-fit

1. **Use a real buildable envelope, not the raw parcel polygon.** The AR-5 area-only screen passes, but exact setbacks, easements, access rights, recorded restrictions, lot coverage, height, and use allowances are not yet known. Do not place buildings on a map and call them zoning-compliant until those constraints and the legal boundary are loaded.
2. **Model each use separately before combining it.** The residence, 30+ day cohort accommodation, AI lab/demo, medical function, wellness, dining, and greenhouse may be treated differently. Preserve the user's no-STR rule; do not assume it resolves the County's classification of the other uses.
3. **Start with one coordinated, compact site diagram.** Reserve clearly labeled zones for buildings, accessible pedestrian circulation, emergency access, utility/service corridors, well/source protection, wastewater/reserve, drainage, and retained trees. This is a coordination overlay—not a surveyed site plan.
4. **Keep accessibility in the layout from the first pass.** Prefer short, continuous routes and avoid unnecessary level changes. Mark every grade, landing, crossing, and door-threshold check as TBD until survey and code criteria are available; don't claim a route compliant based on a schematic.
5. **Protect water sources before fixing amenities.** Make well/source, wastewater, fuel/chemical storage, drainage, and any lab/clinical waste separate explicit layers. Use the applicable official separation criteria once obtained; do not substitute a guessed distance.
6. **Keep the tunnel optional and outside the baseline.** It adds geotechnical, structural, drainage, accessibility, egress, fire/life-safety, and permitting interfaces before it can be tested.
7. **Treat Lighting Zone II as a design constraint, not a known numeric standard.** A low-glare concept is prudent, but photometric and fixture requirements must be checked against the actual ordinance.

## Evidence ledger and status vocabulary

For each future plan line, retain:

- **Claim / element** (e.g., “accessible route between arrival and medical entry”);
- **Evidence class**: county screenshot, user directive, GIS/EPQS observation, calculation, design assumption, field/survey result, official interpretation, or professional-issued document;
- **Source, date, locator, and file hash** where available;
- **Calculation method and inputs** for derived values;
- **Status**: verified for stated purpose, preliminary, assumed, unresolved, or superseded;
- **Reviewer and limits**: who may rely on it and what must still be confirmed.

Do not promote “preliminary,” “assumed,” “screenshot-transcribed,” “GIS,” or “calculated from assumed inputs” to “engineered,” “approved,” or “compliant.” A licensed professional can then verify, revise, adopt, or reject the preparatory work as part of their own scope.
